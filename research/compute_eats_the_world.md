# Compute Eats the World

## 0. Purpose

This note extends, but does not replace, `qualitative_compute_growth.md`.

That paper argues that AI compute can grow qualitatively because buyers increasingly demand not merely raw FLOPs, but usable cognitive capacity with particular properties of timing, locality, latency, resilience, jurisdiction, security, provenance and fallback.

This continuation states a stronger hypothesis:

> **Physical resources are finite, but the economically useful space of computationally mediated qualitative transformations may remain so incompletely explored that qualitative growth can be quasi-unbounded on human economic horizons.**

The qualifier **quasi** is essential. This is not a claim of literal physical infinity.

---

## 1. Historical lineage: software eats the world

In 2011 Marc Andreessen, co-founder of Netscape, published *Why Software Is Eating the World* in *The Wall Street Journal*. His thesis was that software companies and software-mediated services were taking over increasingly large parts of the economy.

The present hypothesis is a continuation rather than a rejection of that observation.

```text
software digitizes and reorganizes processes
→ AI makes more cognitive work computationally addressable
→ inference turns compute into prediction, judgment, design and coordination
→ compute becomes able to absorb a larger fraction of economic activity
```

A provisional formulation is:

> **Software ate processes. Inference makes cognition increasingly computable. Compute therefore eats more of the world.**

This is not a claim that every activity should or will become software, nor that human judgment disappears. It is a claim about the expanding set of activities for which computational capacity becomes a material input to useful action.

---

## 2. Qualitative growth under finite physical constraints

A frequent mistake is to collapse all growth into material throughput.

The stronger thesis distinguishes:

```text
material quantity
≠
quality of organization, knowledge, prediction, design or decision
```

Physical resources remain bounded:

```text
energy < ∞
materials < ∞
land < ∞
time < ∞
```

But the useful combinations that can be explored with them may remain extraordinarily large:

```text
knowledge
× context
× models
× simulations
× designs
× decisions
× coordination patterns
× possible actions
```

Therefore the relevant economic question is not whether matter is infinite. It is whether the frontier of useful qualitative transformation is close to exhaustion.

The working hypothesis is that it is not.

A compact statement is:

> **The alternative to infinite material growth is not necessarily degrowth. It may be quasi-unbounded qualitative growth under finite physical constraints.**

This remains an economic and technological hypothesis, not a demonstrated law.

---

## 3. Inference as a general-purpose conversion layer

Energy enables physical action.

Inference can improve the choice, timing, composition and control of action.

A useful transformation chain is:

```text
energy
→ compute
→ inference
→ prediction / diagnosis / design / decision / coordination
→ improved action
→ new knowledge and capabilities
→ new inference demand
```

This creates a potential positive loop:

```text
more useful inference
→ more discovered uses
→ more background and foreground cognitive work
→ more demand for qualified compute
```

The strategic resource is therefore not simply the accelerator.

It is increasingly:

> **assured cognitive capacity: the ability to produce a useful inference under the required operational, legal, temporal and governance constraints.**

---

## 4. Background work and the absorption of surplus compute

Interactive demand does not exhaust the useful demand for computation.

A cognitive system can maintain a backlog of valuable but deferrable work:

- consolidation;
- indexing;
- replay;
- cognitive regression testing;
- cold-handler tests;
- search;
- simulation;
- verification;
- contradiction detection;
- hypothesis exploration;
- optimization;
- corpus maintenance;
- dreaming / sleep-cycle work.

This creates an economic mechanism by which low-priority work can absorb capacity that would otherwise be unused.

```text
foreground demand < available capacity
→ allocate residual capacity to admissible background work
```

The work must remain governed:

```text
idle ≠ free ≠ authorized
```

Every background packet still requires mandate, bounded budget, priority, preemptibility and traceability.

The important implication is that falling compute cost may reveal new useful work faster than it suppresses demand for existing work.

This is a Jevons-like rebound hypothesis, not an assertion that utilization must physically remain at 100%.

Reserved headroom may itself be a valuable product.

---

## 5. Scarcity moves rather than disappears

If raw compute becomes cheaper, scarcity need not vanish.

It can migrate toward qualified capability:

```text
compute now
compute here
compute with low tail latency
compute under an accepted jurisdiction
compute with confidentiality
compute with provenance
compute with reserved fallback
compute during a crisis
compute with auditable execution
```

This preserves the main result of `qualitative_compute_growth.md`:

> **raw compute abundance can coexist with scarcity of qualified cognitive capacity.**

Hence the premium market does not need permanent aggregate GPU scarcity.

It only requires scarce combinations of properties that matter to the buyer.

---

## 6. The "black gold" analogy

Oil became strategically important not merely because it was scarce, but because it was a highly fungible source of energy that could be transformed into many forms of capability.

Inference may become analogous at the information layer:

```text
oil
→ mobility / heat / industry / chemistry

inference
→ prediction / diagnosis / design / coordination / automation / discovery
```

The analogy has limits. Inference remains dependent on physical energy, materials, networks and human institutions.

But the strategic proposition is useful:

> **The black gold of an inference economy is not the token itself; it is assured inference capacity.**

---

## 7. Corsica and MareNostrum: from gold vault to inference vault

The Swiss analogy should be framed institutionally rather than geologically.

Switzerland did not become a financial center because it mined most of the assets it protected. It developed a position around custody, security, law, trust, infrastructure, financial expertise and cross-border asset management.

The MareNostrum hypothesis is analogous:

```text
energy access
+ secure compute
+ territorial topology
+ resilient connectivity
+ jurisdiction
+ governance
+ provenance
+ fallback
→ trusted inference custody and execution
```

Corsica, associated with other Mediterranean territories and infrastructures through MareNostrum, could test a role as a **vault for cognitive assets and assured inference capacity**.

Candidate protected or executable assets include:

- models;
- datasets;
- cryptographic keys;
- digital-twin memories;
- reserved compute capacity;
- execution proofs;
- Cognitive Packets and continuations;
- replicated corpora;
- emergency inference capability.

The product is not "Corsican GPU-hours".

It is closer to:

> **the ability to preserve and execute cognitive capacity under explicit guarantees of security, continuity, sovereignty, provenance and fallback.**

This is a hypothesis for experiment, not a claim that Corsica presently has these capabilities at scale.

---

## 8. Relation to degrowth arguments

This note does not deny ecological limits.

It rejects a narrower equivalence:

```text
economic growth = proportional growth of material throughput
```

A more useful decomposition is:

```text
growth
= quantitative growth
+ qualitative growth
```

Quantitative growth encounters direct physical constraints.

Qualitative growth can arise from better knowledge, organization, design, coordination and use of finite resources.

Compute and inference may accelerate this second component by making more of the space of possible transformations searchable and actionable.

However, rebound effects remain first-class objections. Better efficiency can increase aggregate demand and therefore total energy and material consumption.

The thesis is therefore not:

> "AI abolishes physical limits."

It is:

> **"Physical limits do not imply that the economically useful qualitative frontier is close to exhaustion."**

---

## 9. Falsifiable hypotheses

### CEW1 — Expanding computational addressability

The share of economically valuable activities in which inference becomes a material input continues to increase.

**Falsifier:** AI/inference adoption plateaus broadly because most remaining activities show negligible value from additional computational mediation.

### CEW2 — Qualitative frontier remains open

Efficiency improvements and new models continue to reveal economically useful tasks, combinations and service qualities faster than existing categories are exhausted.

**Falsifier:** sustained improvements in compute and model efficiency produce broad structural saturation of useful inference demand.

### CEW3 — Background absorption

Deferrable cognitive work absorbs a material share of otherwise idle compute when systems expose bounded background scheduling.

**Falsifier:** mature systems routinely leave economically usable compute idle because the backlog of worthwhile deferrable cognitive work is exhausted.

### CEW4 — Qualified scarcity persists

Price premiums persist for at least some combinations of latency, resilience, sovereignty, confidentiality, provenance, reservation and fallback even as commodity inference prices fall.

**Falsifier:** such properties become broadly substitutable and command negligible persistent premiums.

### CEW5 — Territorial inference-vault premium

Some customers value geographically and institutionally bounded inference custody/execution enough to pay for a territorial or federated sovereign service.

**Falsifier:** hyperscaler offerings fully substitute for territorial trust, resilience and governance across the targeted workloads at lower total cost and equal accepted risk.

---

## 10. Empirical program

Priority evidence should include:

1. the fraction of AI demand attributable to new use cases versus substitution of existing workloads;
2. utilization and pricing differences between foreground, scheduled background and opportunistic background compute;
3. observed willingness to pay for priority and for interruptibility discounts;
4. price premiums for reservation, latency, sovereignty, provenance and fallback;
5. energy consumption per useful inference and total inference-driven energy demand;
6. rebound effects following large efficiency improvements;
7. demand for secure model/data/key custody tied to local or sovereign execution;
8. customer willingness to pay for geographically bounded, auditable inference;
9. whether background learning workloads measurably increase network utilization without degrading foreground SLAs;
10. whether such background work increases durable capability rather than merely consuming compute.

---

## 11. Claim discipline

This note does **not** claim:

- literal infinite economic growth;
- infinite energy or matter;
- inevitable 100% compute utilization;
- that every new inference has positive social value;
- that more compute automatically creates more welfare;
- that rebound effects are harmless;
- that Corsica already constitutes an inference safe haven;
- that territorial infrastructure must replace hyperscalers;
- that qualified compute scarcity is permanent for every workload.

The central proposition is narrower:

> **Compute can mediate an expanding space of qualitative transformations, and inference can turn this expansion into sustained demand for increasingly differentiated cognitive capacity.**

## 12. External lineage

- Marc Andreessen, *Why Software Is Eating the World*, *The Wall Street Journal*, 20 August 2011; archived by Andreessen Horowitz.
- Paul M. Romer, *Endogenous Technological Change*, *Journal of Political Economy*, 1990 — non-rival ideas and endogenous growth are important economic ancestors of the qualitative-growth hypothesis.
- William Stanley Jevons, *The Coal Question*, 1865 — historical ancestor of the rebound problem: greater efficiency can expand rather than contract aggregate resource use.

These references provide lineage, not proof of the present thesis.
